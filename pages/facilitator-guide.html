Facilitator Guide · Phase I · Foundations

Running a quest with your team

A one-page guide for leading the .LLab Foundation Strength Snapshot™ in a single 45-minute meeting, so the structure and the language stay the same every time.

Open the quest →

Before · 10 minutes

Prepare the room

  • Invite three to eight leaders who share responsibility for the organization.
  • Send each person the quest link a day ahead and ask them not to start it yet.
  • Name one facilitator and one note-taker. The facilitator does not score.
  • Have the four dimensions visible to everyone: what you are building and why, priorities in action, the structures holding you up, and your next right move.

Open · 5 minutes

Set the frame

  • State the purpose: to see where the foundation is strong and where it is thin, not to judge anyone.
  • Agree that every score is honest and individual, and that differences are useful.
  • Remind the room that Foundations come first in The IV Phases™: everything else leans on them.

Score · 10 minutes

Answer alone

  • Each leader opens the quest and scores the four dimensions silently, 1 to 5.
  • Each writes their own closing commitment.
  • With team mode, the inviting member sends the invitations from the quest page, and everyone sees the team view once they have answered.

Compare · 15 minutes

Find the gaps

  • Reveal the scores for one dimension at a time.
  • Where scores differ by two or more, ask the highest and the lowest scorer what they see.
  • Ask of the lowest dimension: what would have to be true for this to score a 4?

Commit · 5 minutes

Leave with one action

  • Agree on one shared commitment for the lowest dimension: the action, its owner and its date.
  • The note-taker records it where the whole team can see it.

After · 30 days

Come back to it

  • Each leader receives a check-in on their commitment 30 days after finishing the quest.
  • Open the next meeting with one question: what happened?
  • When the foundation is steady, move to a Phase II quest with the same team.

Language to keep

Say infrastructure, not problems; strength to build from, not weakness; the next right move, not the fix. The quest names a strength before it names a gap, and so should the facilitator.
