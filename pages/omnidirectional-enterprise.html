Legacy, powered by Omnidirectional Enterprise™

The legacy of interconnected enterprises.

Omnidirectional Enterprise™ is an economic enterprise system. It teaches enterprises how to build a legacy, and helps enterprises that are still a dream become real, through collaborative support and a network that connects every sector.

The problem The idea The goal The model The blueprint The network Why it lasts The business model Where you fit in

The problem

Value is created everywhere. It is captured in few places.

Capital, labor, intellectual property, technology, and decision-making often run through systems whose incentives do not match who contributes. Returns from innovation concentrate, regions are left out, and enterprises falter when the founder steps back.

Most enterprises are also built alone: each one writes its own playbook, finds its own capital, and keeps its returns inside its own walls.

The idea

Every enterprise, on equal footing.

We say enterprise, not business. Small business, corporation, nonprofit: those are tax structures, not ranks. Omnidirectional Enterprise™ connects enterprises of every kind as collaborators, so value moves in every direction instead of up a ladder.

Within

Revenue flows inside each enterprise

Every enterprise runs on one shared foundation: licensed IP and The IV Phases™, one common way to decide and organize.

Across

Then out across sectors

Interconnections let products, services, ideas, and revenue move directly between enterprises in different sectors.

Into a web

A network that sustains communities

What grows is a web of exchange, designed to sustain communities from local to global.

Old-school, honorable business ethics, with modern economic impact.

The goal and the outcome

Leadership Infrastructure™, modelled first hand.

The goal

A network that works as one system

To model, first hand, what Leadership Infrastructure™ means: a network of enterprises that do not operate independently, but as one system.

The outcome

Revenue that holds across the network

To support the evolution of economic enterprise: generating and sustaining revenue across the economic network, with value that holds across geopolitical lines instead of depending on any one of them.

How we measure it.

Qualitative

What changes

  • Enterprises collaborate as peers, across sectors.
  • Leadership is documented and passed on, not lost with one person.
  • Communities are sustained where enterprises operate.
  • Ethics are held as part of the infrastructure.
Quantitative

What we count

  • Enterprises in the network.
  • Sectors and regions connected.
  • Revenue flowing between enterprises in the network.
  • Share of returns reinvested.
  • Enterprises completing each of The IV Phases™, through Preservation.

Measured as the network grows, not promised in advance.

Why build inside it — How it works and what each branch gains.

Why build inside it

An economic enterprise system, not another program.

Most enterprises are built alone. Each one writes its own playbook, looks for its own capital, protects its own ideas, and keeps its returns inside its own walls. That is slow and costly, and it leaves the enterprise exposed when the founder steps back, people change, or the market shifts.

Omnidirectional Enterprise™ treats enterprise as a system. The foundations, governance, knowledge, connections, and reinvestment are built once, shared across every participant, and kept moving, so each enterprise builds on what the others have already built. The mechanisms that make it work:

Shared IP

Ideas you license and put to work

Instead of inventing every framework, tool, and process from scratch, an enterprise licenses intellectual property from the Source and applies it to what it already does best. What it learns flows back through the Living IP Current, so the IP keeps improving for every enterprise that uses it. Ideas, unlike physical goods, can be used by many people at once.

One governance model

One way to decide and organize

Every participant builds from the same playbook: The IV Phases™ (Foundations, Planning, Operating, and Preservation). Enterprises in different sectors share the same language for decisions, so they can work together without starting over each time. Preservation plans for the moment the founder steps back, people change, or the market shifts.

A cross-sector network

Connections in every direction

Enterprises, nonprofits, creators, and communities build side by side, linked by Interconnections. With a license or Membership, an enterprise can join The .LLab Collaborating Companies, where other enterprises in the system can find it and work with it.

Revenue reinvested

Returns that keep moving

Returns are reinvested across the portfolio to fund what comes next. Partnership investment powers the Source through the Investment Current, and impact allocation, giving, and charity support reach Nonprofits through the Impact Current. Value keeps moving to everyone who helps create it.

What changes — How enterprises are built, supported and expanded.

What changes

How enterprise systems are built, supported, and expanded.

Built

From one shared foundation

Every enterprise starts from the same foundation: the intellectual property of the Source and The IV Phases™ (Foundations, Planning, Operating, and Preservation). An enterprise takes in that foundation and applies it to what it already does best, becoming a Conductor in the system instead of building alone from nothing.

Supported

From every direction

An enterprise draws from the Source through its Transmission line and exchanges directly with other enterprises through Interconnections. Help, knowledge, and collaboration do not have to pass through one centre; they reach each enterprise from many sides at once.

Expanded

Outward, one generation at a time

Each Conductor runs the same model for the enterprises, creators, and communities it serves. The web keeps branching outward, and every new enterprise joins the same connected system rather than starting a separate one.

Between the branches — How impact flows from sector to sector.

Between the branches

How impact flows from sector to sector.

The figures name branches, each a sector where economic activity happens: Enterprises, Nonprofits, Communities, People, Products & Services, Technology, Capital, Knowledge + IP, and Creators. The IV Phases™ give every branch one common way to decide and organize. Lines carry value between them:

Source ↔ branch

Transmission lines

Carry power from the Source out to each branch and carry value back. Two-way.

Branch ↔ branch

Interconnections

Let two branches, or two enterprises, exchange directly without going through the Source. This is what makes the model omnidirectional.

Named flows

Currents

Named flows of value in a set direction. Figure 2 shows them, from the Investment Current to the Impact Current.

Because every branch is linked to the Source and to its neighbours, impact made in one sector can reach the others. Partnership investment from Enterprises and Capital powers the Source; the Source licenses its IP back out through Knowledge + IP and Products & Services; enterprises return what they learn, strengthening the IP; creators' work reaches Communities; and impact allocation, giving, and charity support reach Nonprofits.

Research and economic foundations — The published ideas the model builds on.

Research and economic foundations

The published ideas the model builds on.

These are independent, published works in economics and management. They explain why coordination, governance, intellectual property and ecosystems matter to economic outcomes, the same questions Omnidirectional Enterprise™ is built around. The summaries are our own plain-language readings. The authors and publishers have not endorsed The Co.LLab™, and the works are not evidence that the model produces any particular result.

Ronald Coase

The Nature of the Firm

Economica, 1937

Organizations exist because using markets has costs. When coordinating inside an organization is cheaper than contracting for each piece, the organization is the better way to coordinate resources.

Oliver Williamson

The Economic Institutions of Capitalism

Free Press, 1985

How an activity is governed affects its economic result. Shared rules and clear decision rights lower the cost of working together. Williamson shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Economics for this line of work.

Elinor Ostrom

Governing the Commons

Cambridge University Press, 1990

Shared resources can be managed well by the people who use them, when rules, roles and monitoring are clear and the rules fit local conditions. Ostrom shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Economics.

David Teece

Profiting from Innovation

Research Policy, 1986

Creating an innovation and capturing its returns are different problems. Who profits depends on ownership of the idea and on the complementary assets needed to bring it to market.

Paul Romer

Endogenous Technological Change

Journal of Political Economy, 1990

Ideas, unlike physical goods, can be used by many people at once, and investment in new ideas drives long-run growth. Romer received the 2018 Nobel Prize in Economics for work on this.

Jonathan Haskel and Stian Westlake

Capitalism without Capital

Princeton University Press, 2017

Investment in intangible assets such as intellectual property, software, design and organizational know-how is a large and growing part of how modern economies create value, and intangibles behave differently from physical assets.

James Moore

Predators and Prey: A New Ecology of Competition

Harvard Enterprise Review, 1993

Enterprises can be understood as members of enterprise ecosystems, where participants from different sectors coevolve around shared capabilities rather than competing as isolated firms.
